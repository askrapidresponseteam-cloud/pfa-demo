If the station will not write it

A refusal is a step, not the end.

Each rung has a provision behind it and a paper trail that carries to the next. Take the acknowledgement at every stage, however grudgingly it is given: the refusal is what makes the following step available.

  1. The station house officer

    Cruelty that kills or maims is cognizable, so information about it must be recorded. Ask for the FIR number and a copy in writing before you leave. If the officer records it as a non-cognizable report instead, say which section you are relying on and ask that the refusal be noted.

    BNSS 2023 s.173 · BNS 2023 s.325

  2. The Superintendent of Police

    Where the station refuses, the same information goes to the SP in writing, by post or by email, with the date and the name of the officer who refused. The SP may investigate or direct an investigation.

    BNSS 2023 s.173(4)

  3. The Judicial Magistrate

    If the SP does nothing either, move the Magistrate for a direction to investigate. The two earlier refusals are the record the application rests on, which is why the acknowledgements matter more than the arguing did.

    BNSS 2023 s.175(3)

  4. Custody of the animal, immediately

    This does not wait for the FIR. Animals seized in a cruelty case can be entrusted to an infirmary, gaushala or recognised welfare organisation for the duration of the trial. Move it the same day: an animal returned to the person accused of hurting it rarely survives the wait.

    Case Property Animals Rules 2017 · PCA 1960 s.35

  5. The District Magistrate and the district SPCA

    Where the failure is the district's rather than one officer's - no working ABC centre, no SPCA constituted, seized cattle with nowhere to go - it is the DM who answers for it, and the SPCA he chairs that was supposed to exist.

    PCA 1960 s.4 · SPCA Rules 2001 · ABC Rules 2023

  6. The High Court

    A standing failure by a State body is a writ, and animal welfare organisations have long been heard on one. Anyone may give information about a cognizable offence; you do not need to be the owner or an organisation to start any of this.

    Art 226 · AWBI v. Nagaraja (2014) 7 SCC 547

Put it on the record

One case is an incident. Four hundred is a pattern a State has to answer.

What you file here joins the district's count. The count is the argument: a District Magistrate can dismiss a complaint, and cannot as easily dismiss the number of them from his own district with no ABC centre to show against it.

Your report stays private to PFA, as every report on this site does. Only the district totals are ever published - never the footage, the address, your name, or the name of anyone you accuse. Nobody has been convicted at the point you file, and a public allegation would put both of you at risk.

So a named person at PFA can reach you, and so you can follow the reference this gives you.

A refusal is worth recording even if you go no further. It is the number of refusals in a district that eventually moves anything.

Video is the evidence

A clip with the date, the place and the animal in one continuous shot is worth more than any description. Up to 3 files.

    Take it upward

    A letter with your district's own numbers in it.

    Addressed to the District Magistrate and copied to the sitting MLA. It asks for the three things a district is actually obliged to have, and it cites what obliges them. Fill in the two names and send it; a letter that names the provision gets a file opened, where a letter that expresses concern gets a receipt.

    On your district's NIC site, usually under Administration.

    On your State Legislative Assembly's members list.

    Every section named on this page is one of the two hundred answers on Animal laws in India, which names the provision each rests on and was last read against the statutes in August 2026. Read the section on indiacode.nic.in before you quote it, and check your State: prevention of cruelty is a concurrent subject, so State rules and municipal by-laws sit on top of everything here. A section cited wrongly in an FIR is harder to correct than one left out.
