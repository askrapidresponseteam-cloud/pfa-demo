Leave a legacy

Remember the animals in your will.

A gift in your will reaches animals long after you, and it costs nothing today. Find the wording to take to your lawyer, or talk to PFA in confidence. Nothing you write here commits you.

Where it began

One will. Forty-six years of care.

In 1980, Mrs Ruth Cowell of New South Wales, Australia, left a legacy to Shri Sanjay Gandhi for the care of animals. It built the Sanjay Gandhi Animal Care Centre in Raja Garden, New Delhi, which today looks after more than three thousand animals at any one time.

She never saw the animals her gift would care for. Every one of them is her legacy.

Ways to give

Five ways a will can remember them.

Provide for the people you love first. Then, if you wish, the animals.

  • A share of what you leave

    A percentage of your estate, once your family and loved ones are provided for. It keeps its value however the years change prices.

  • A sum of money

    A fixed amount, named in your will.

  • Property or another asset

    A house, land, shares, jewellery or another asset, named in your will.

  • In memory of someone

    A gift in the name of a person, or an animal, you loved.

  • A place with your name

    PFA names a facility after a donor who leaves it a legacy, so the gift is remembered where it does its work.

  • Not sure yet?

    Most people are not, at first. Talk it through with PFA, privately, before you decide anything.

How a will works

Four steps, with a lawyer beside you.

  1. List what you ownProperty, savings, investments, vehicles and valuables.
  2. Decide who receives whatThe people you love, and any cause you want to remember.
  3. Sign it before two witnessesThey sign too. Registering a will is optional in India, but keeps it safe.
  4. Keep it safe, and review itWith your lawyer or someone you trust. Look at it again after a marriage, a birth or a move.

General information, not legal advice. Personal law can change what applies to you; a lawyer will tell you what does.
